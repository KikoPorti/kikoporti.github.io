Neural Language Processing

What EEG, MEG, and predictive brain responses reveal about the real-time architecture of language processing.

Context

Comprehension unfolds in real time, and the brain appears to generate predictions ahead of incoming linguistic input — a process that computational surprisal tries to approximate. Neural measures like EEG and MEG offer a much finer-grained window into that process than behavioral measures alone, capturing prediction and integration as they happen, millisecond by millisecond, rather than only at the endpoint of a response.

My approach

At the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics, I work on time-frequency analysis of prosodic timing using Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) in MATLAB, comparing German and French. Alongside this, I participate in live EEG and eye-tracking experiments at BCBL, and read extensively in EEG/MEG methodology to connect predictive brain responses back to the same surprisal-based framework I use in my language acquisition and computational modeling work.
