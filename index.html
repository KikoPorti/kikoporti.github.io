01 Research

I study how humans acquire, represent, and process language, and what computational models can reveal about the mechanisms underlying linguistic cognition.

01.1

Language Acquisition

How children acquire language from limited input, with a focus on predictive processing, cloze probability, and the developmental trajectory of linguistic knowledge in Spanish-speaking children.

01.2

Computational Models

Using language models as computational probes of linguistic behavior, examining where their predictions align with human judgments and where systematic divergences emerge.

01.3

Neural Language Processing

The neural and cognitive architecture of language processing, including EEG, MEG, and eye-tracking methodologies, and the relationship between predictive brain responses and computational surprisal.

01.4

Multisensory Speech

Audiovisual integration in speech perception, including the McGurk effect and the extent to which computational models reproduce the stochastic nature of human perceptual fusion.

02 Selected Publications

2026

Frequency Predicts Acquisition, Surprisal Predicts Reading: A Developmental Dissociation in Spanish

Francisco Portillo · Manuscript submitted for publication

Under Review

Investigates the relationship between LLM surprisal, child-directed input frequency, and adult reading times, revealing a developmental dissociation between early word learning and real-time language comprehension.

2026

Artificial Rigidities vs. Biological Noise: A Comparative Analysis of Multisensory Integration in AV-HuBERT and Human Observers

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint

Under Review Preprint

Investigates whether AV-HuBERT replicates the McGurk effect in ways that parallel human audiovisual speech integration. Finds that the model mirrors auditory dominance under conflict but exhibits rigid fusion rates and lacks human-like perceptual stochasticity.

2025

Imperfect Language, Artificial Intelligence, and the Human Mind: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Linguistic Errors in Native Spanish Speakers

Francisco Portillo · arXiv:2511.01615

Preprint

Examines semantic and orthographic error correction patterns across GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA, comparing model behavior with that of native Spanish speakers and identifying systematic asymmetries between human and artificial linguistic processing.
