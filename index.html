Research

My research asks how linguistic knowledge emerges, how it is represented and processed by the brain, and what computational models can—and cannot— tell us about these mechanisms.

01

Cognitive plausibility of LLMs

Testing whether language models reproduce human linguistic behavior, and identifying systematic parallels and divergences between artificial and biological language processing.

02

Language in the brain

Studying where and how linguistic knowledge is represented and processed in the brain, with an interest in the relationship between computational predictions and neural signatures.

03

Language acquisition

Investigating how children acquire linguistic knowledge from limited input, including predictive processing, cloze probability, surprisal, entropy, and developmental change.

04

Multisensory speech

Examining audiovisual speech perception and the McGurk effect, including whether computational models capture the stochastic character of human perceptual integration.

Methods & tools

EEG · MEG · Eye-tracking · Behavioral experiments · ELAN · Praat · Phon · Python · R · MATLAB · NLP · Language models · Surprisal · Entropy · Cloze probability · Time-frequency analysis

Selected Publications

2026

Frequency Predicts Acquisition, Surprisal Predicts Reading: A Developmental Dissociation in Spanish

Francisco Portillo · Manuscript submitted for publication

Under Review

Investigates the relationship between LLM surprisal, child-directed input frequency, and adult reading times, revealing a developmental dissociation between early word learning and real-time language comprehension.

2026

Artificial Rigidities vs. Biological Noise: A Comparative Analysis of Multisensory Integration in AV-HuBERT and Human Observers

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint

Under Review Preprint

Investigates whether AV-HuBERT replicates the McGurk effect in ways that parallel human audiovisual speech integration. Finds auditory dominance under conflict alongside rigid fusion rates and limited human-like perceptual stochasticity.

2025

Imperfect Language, Artificial Intelligence, and the Human Mind: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Linguistic Errors in Native Spanish Speakers

Francisco Portillo · arXiv:2511.01615

Preprint

Examines semantic and orthographic error correction patterns across GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA, comparing model behavior with that of native Spanish speakers and identifying systematic asymmetries between human and artificial linguistic processing.
