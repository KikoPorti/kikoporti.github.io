Fourth-year undergraduate in Spanish Language and Literature at Universidad de Navarra, preparing for doctoral study in linguistics.

Currently: Research Intern at Harvard PhonLab & BCBL

I study how language is acquired and processed in the human brain, and whether large language models provide cognitively plausible models of these processes. I am particularly interested in the parallels and divergences between artificial and human language processing, and in whether computational models can help us understand how and where language is represented in the brain.

Research

My work asks what computational models can tell us about the cognitive architecture of language — especially when their behavior converges with, or systematically departs from, human language processing.

01

Cognitive Plausibility of LLMs

Using language models as experimental probes: testing whether their predictions and representations resemble human linguistic behavior, and identifying systematic divergences.

02

Language in the Brain

Studying where and how linguistic information is represented and processed in the brain, linking behavioral measures with EEG, MEG, eye-tracking, and computational surprisal.

03

Language Acquisition

Investigating how linguistic knowledge emerges from limited input, with particular interest in prediction, frequency, surprisal, and the developmental trajectory of Spanish-speaking children.

04

Multisensory Speech

Examining audiovisual speech perception and the McGurk effect, including whether computational models reproduce the flexible and probabilistic nature of human perceptual integration.

Selected Publications

Frequency Predicts Acquisition, Surprisal Predicts Reading: A Developmental Dissociation in Spanish

Francisco Portillo · Manuscript submitted for publication

Under Review

Investigates the relationship between LLM surprisal, child-directed input frequency, and adult reading times, revealing a developmental dissociation between early word learning and real-time language comprehension.

2026

Artificial Rigidities vs. Biological Noise: A Comparative Analysis of Multisensory Integration in AV-HuBERT and Human Observers ↗

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint

Under Review Preprint

Investigates whether AV-HuBERT replicates the McGurk effect in ways that parallel human audiovisual speech integration. Finds auditory dominance under conflict alongside more rigid fusion patterns than those observed in humans.

2025

Imperfect Language, Artificial Intelligence, and the Human Mind: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Linguistic Errors in Native Spanish Speakers ↗

Francisco Portillo · arXiv:2511.01615

Preprint

Examines semantic and orthographic error correction patterns across GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA, comparing model behavior with that of native Spanish speakers.
