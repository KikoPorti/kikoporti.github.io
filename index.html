I study how language is acquired and processed in the human brain, and whether computational models can help explain the cognitive architecture that makes language possible.

My work focuses on the cognitive plausibility of large language models: identifying where their representations and predictions parallel human language behavior, where they diverge, and whether those patterns can provide insight into how language is represented and processed in the brain.

Research

I am interested in the relationship between linguistic computation and the human language system — from early acquisition to real-time processing and neural representation.

01

Cognitive plausibility of LLMs

When do language models behave like humans, and what do systematic similarities and divergences tell us about linguistic cognition?

02

Language in the brain

How is linguistic knowledge represented and processed in the brain, and how can behavioral and neural signatures constrain computational accounts?

03

Language acquisition

How learners extract structure from input, and how frequency, surprisal, prediction, and experience shape developing linguistic representations.

04

Computational neuroscience

Using computational measures such as surprisal and entropy alongside EEG, MEG, eye-tracking, and behavioral data.

Selected publications

2026

Frequency Predicts Acquisition, Surprisal Predicts Reading: A Developmental Dissociation in Spanish

Francisco Portillo · Manuscript submitted for publication

Investigates the relationship between language-model surprisal, child-directed input frequency, and adult reading times, revealing a developmental dissociation between early word learning and real-time comprehension.

2026

Artificial Rigidities vs. Biological Noise: A Comparative Analysis of Multisensory Integration in AV-HuBERT and Human Observers

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint · Under Review

Examines whether AV-HuBERT reproduces human audiovisual speech integration, with particular attention to the McGurk effect, auditory dominance, and perceptual variability.

2025

Imperfect Language, Artificial Intelligence, and the Human Mind: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Linguistic Errors in Native Spanish Speakers

Francisco Portillo · arXiv:2511.01615 · Preprint

Compares semantic and orthographic error correction patterns across GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA with patterns observed in native Spanish speakers.
