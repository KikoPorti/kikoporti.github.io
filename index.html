Linguistics & Cognitive Science

Francisco Portillo

I am a fourth-year undergraduate in Spanish Language and Literature at the Universidad de Navarra, currently a Research Intern at the Harvard Phonetics Laboratory (under Dr. Kathryn Franich) and at the BCBL (Basque Center on Cognition, Brain and Language), and Research Assistant at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics and the Clinical Linguistics and Disparities Lab (University of Iowa).

My research sits at the intersection of language acquisition, neurolinguistics, and computational linguistics. I use computational models — particularly large language models — as experimental mirrors to study what their alignment with, and divergence from, human behavior reveals about the cognitive architecture of language.

Research Interests

Language Acquisition

How children acquire language from limited input, with a focus on predictive processing, cloze probability, and the developmental trajectory of linguistic knowledge in Spanish-speaking children.

Computational Linguistics & NLP

Using large language models as cognitive probes — evaluating where their behavior aligns with human linguistic judgments and where systematic divergences reveal uniquely biological constraints.

Neurolinguistics

The neural and cognitive architecture of language processing, including EEG/MEG and eye-tracking methodologies, and the relationship between predictive brain responses and computational surprisal measures.

Multisensory Speech Perception

Audiovisual integration in speech perception, including the McGurk effect and how state-of-the-art audiovisual models replicate — or fail to replicate — the stochastic nature of human perceptual fusion.

Frequency Predicts Acquisition, Surprisal Predicts Reading: A Developmental Dissociation in Spanish

Francisco Portillo · Manuscript submitted for publication

Under Review

Investigates the relationship between LLM surprisal, child-directed input frequency, and adult reading times, revealing a systematic developmental dissociation between early word learning and real-time language comprehension.

2026

Artificial Rigidities vs. Biological Noise: A Comparative Analysis of Multisensory Integration in AV-HuBERT and Human Observers

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint

Under Review Preprint

Investigates whether AV-HuBERT replicates the McGurk effect in ways that parallel human audiovisual speech integration. Finds that the model mirrors auditory dominance under conflict but exhibits rigid fusion rates and lacks human-like perceptual stochasticity.

2025

Imperfect Language, Artificial Intelligence, and the Human Mind: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Linguistic Errors in Native Spanish Speakers

Francisco Portillo · arXiv:2511.01615

Preprint

Examines semantic and orthographic error correction patterns across GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA, comparing model behavior with that of native Spanish speakers and identifying systematic asymmetries between human and artificial linguistic processing.
