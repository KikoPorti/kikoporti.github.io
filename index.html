Undergraduate researcher at the intersection of language acquisition, neurolinguistics, and computational linguistics.

I study the cognitive plausibility of LLMs as models of human language, examining their parallels and divergences with human language acquisition and processing, and exploring whether their computational representations can offer insight into how and where language is represented and processed in the brain.

01

Research

Questions about language,
models, and the mind.

My work asks how linguistic knowledge emerges from experience and how computational models can be used as experimental tools for studying human language cognition.

01 /

Language Acquisition

How children acquire language from limited input, with a focus on predictive processing, cloze probability, and developmental trajectories in Spanish-speaking children.

02 /

Computational Models

Using language models as computational probes of linguistic behavior and examining where their predictions align with human judgments—and where they diverge.

03 /

Neural Language Processing

The neural and cognitive architecture of language processing, including EEG, MEG, eye-tracking, and the relationship between neural responses and computational surprisal.

04 /

Multisensory Speech

Audiovisual integration in speech perception, including the McGurk effect and the extent to which computational models reproduce human perceptual variability.

Selected publications

Research in progress.

2026

UNDER REVIEW

Frequency Predicts Acquisition, Surprisal Predicts Reading: A Developmental Dissociation in Spanish

Francisco Portillo · Manuscript submitted for publication

Investigates the relationship between LLM surprisal, child-directed input frequency, and adult reading times, revealing a developmental dissociation between early word learning and real-time language comprehension.

2026

PREPRINT · UNDER REVIEW

Artificial Rigidities vs. Biological Noise: A Comparative Analysis of Multisensory Integration in AV-HuBERT and Human Observers ↗

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint

Investigates whether AV-HuBERT replicates the McGurk effect in ways that parallel human audiovisual speech integration, with particular attention to perceptual stochasticity.

2025

PREPRINT

Imperfect Language, Artificial Intelligence, and the Human Mind: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Linguistic Errors in Native Spanish Speakers ↗

Francisco Portillo · arXiv:2511.01615

Examines semantic and orthographic error correction patterns across GPT-4, Gemini, and LLaMA, comparing model behavior with native Spanish speakers.
