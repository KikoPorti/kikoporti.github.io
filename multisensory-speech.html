Multisensory Speech

Audiovisual integration in speech perception — and whether computational models reproduce the stochastic, noisy nature of human fusion.

Context

Speech perception is not a purely auditory process: what we see on a speaker's face changes what we hear, most famously in the McGurk effect. Human audiovisual fusion is notably stochastic — the same conflicting audiovisual input doesn't always produce the same percept, even in the same listener. That variability is itself informative, and it raises the question of whether audiovisual speech models trained on large corpora reproduce it, or instead converge on rigid, deterministic fusion behavior.

My approach

My work compares AV-HuBERT's behavior under audiovisual conflict to human observers, testing whether the model replicates auditory dominance patterns seen in humans, and whether its fusion rates show anything resembling the perceptual noise that characterizes biological perception — or whether that noise gets smoothed away during training.
